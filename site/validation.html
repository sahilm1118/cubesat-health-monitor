Validation & robustness

Stress-testing the AI beyond its test set

A good test score isn't enough for a satellite. These checks ask whether the probabilities can be trusted, how the system handles fault types it was never shown, and whether the method works on a different spacecraft.

Can the probabilities be trusted?

A reliability diagram. Each point compares what the model predicted with how often the window really was a fault, on the test split. Points on the dashed diagonal mean "p = 0.8" really means about 80%. The calibration curve is fitted on out-of-fold training predictions only.

Injected faults: what gets caught?

Known faults were injected into real, healthy OPS-SAT windows from the test split, one of each type per window, and each window was judged on its own. The AI rarely flags level shifts and amplitude excursions (amber), because its training labels contain few of them. That is why the deployed system pairs it with classic limit checks. See the autonomy section.

Is the anomaly type named correctly?

Among injected faults that the AI flagged, the share whose type was named correctly. These results use a fresh random seed that played no part in developing the type rules.

Limitations & future work

What this project does not yet show

Limitations

  • Small, uneven flight dataset. OPSSAT-AD covers 9 channels on 14 recording days, and half of it comes from a single day (2 June 2022).
  • The AI only knows labelled fault types. It rarely flags level shifts or amplitude excursions, because the training labels contain few of them. The hybrid design with classic limits covers this gap.
  • Anomaly types are inferred. They are validated on synthetic injected faults, not on expert-typed real faults.
  • Fault management is simulated. The state machine runs on real telemetry with injected faults, but it is not flight software and has not been tested on hardware.
  • Battery data is from the lab. Cells were cycled at constant load and temperature, not with orbit-like partial eclipse discharges. Interval coverage is uneven: one cold cell (B0055) is badly under-covered.
  • Models don't transfer between spacecraft. On ESA Mission 1, the OPS-SAT model scored at chance until it was retrained, and the retrained model still catches only about 28% of labelled events. The test covers just 6 univariate channels.

Future work

  • Train on synthetic level-shift and amplitude faults so the AI covers them without relying on limits.
  • Fuse channels: judge the three magnetometer axes and six photodiodes together, not one at a time.
  • Detect concept drift and adapt the channel profiles as the spacecraft ages.
  • Validate battery models on orbit-like cycling data with partial depth of discharge and thermal swings.
  • Formally verify the fault-management state machine, for example with model checking, before any flight use.
  • Extend the cross-mission test to all ESA-ADB channels and missions, with multivariate models.
