AI-Based Autonomous Fault Detection and Predictive Health Monitoring for CubeSats

A CubeSat spends most of each orbit out of contact with its ground station. This system watches the spacecraft's own telemetry on board, flags anomalies as they happen, and forecasts how many charge cycles the battery has left, so the satellite can protect itself before a fault turns into a failure.

The problem

Small satellites can't wait for the ground to notice something is wrong

Short contact windows

A low-Earth-orbit CubeSat is usually visible to its ground station for only a few minutes per pass. A fault that starts between passes can run for hours before an operator sees it.

Little redundancy

A 10 cm unit has no room for backup systems. One drifting sensor or a degrading battery cell can end the mission, so problems have to be caught early.

Thresholds miss subtle faults

Fixed upper and lower limits only catch values that leave their range. Many real anomalies stay in range but change shape: spikes, flat-lines, noise bursts and drift. Learned models catch these.
